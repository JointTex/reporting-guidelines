---
name: guideline-select
description: Work out which reporting guideline and extensions apply to a study from its design, and where to get the official checklist.
---

Use this when the collaborator asks which reporting guideline their manuscript needs, or before a check when the design is not obvious.

1. Read the title, abstract and methods and state the design in one sentence: what was done to whom, how participants or units were assigned or selected, what was compared, and whether this is a completed study, a protocol or an abstract. If the manuscript's label for its design does not match its methods, say so and confirm with the collaborator before going on.
2. Ask for the target journal and read its instructions for authors if the collaborator gives the address (fetch_url). The journal's requirement decides the guideline and the version; some journals also want the completed checklist uploaded as a separate file.
3. Match the design to a guideline. This list is a starting point for the search, not the authority:
   - randomised trial: CONSORT; its protocol: SPIRIT;
   - systematic review or meta-analysis: PRISMA; its protocol: PRISMA-P; scoping review: PRISMA-ScR; meta-analysis of observational studies: MOOSE where the journal asks for it;
   - cohort, case-control or cross-sectional study: STROBE; with routinely collected health data: RECORD;
   - diagnostic accuracy study: STARD;
   - development or validation of a prediction model: TRIPOD;
   - case report: CARE;
   - animal research: ARRIVE;
   - qualitative research: SRQR, or COREQ for interviews and focus groups; synthesis of qualitative research: ENTREQ;
   - non-randomised evaluation of an intervention: TREND;
   - quality improvement study: SQUIRE;
   - economic evaluation: CHEERS;
   - clinical practice guideline: AGREE or RIGHT;
   - description of an intervention within any of these: TIDieR.
4. Confirm the match and find extensions in the EQUATOR Network library (www.equator-network.org) with fetch_url, or with web_search when it is offered. Look for extensions by design feature (cluster, crossover, non-inferiority, pilot, adaptive), by content (harms, patient-reported outcomes, AI or machine learning methods, equity) and by output (abstracts). Report only guidelines and extensions you found there or the collaborator named; do not assert that an extension exists from memory.
5. A study can need more than one: a trial with an economic evaluation, a diagnostic study that also develops a model, a mixed-methods study. Say which part of the manuscript each one covers.
6. Say when nothing fits well, and name the nearest guideline with what it does not cover, instead of forcing a match.
7. Report: the design as you read it, the guideline with its current version and the version the journal asks for if different, the applicable extensions, where each official checklist can be obtained, and what the journal wants submitted. Then offer the matching check skill from this set (`consort-check`, `spirit-check`, `prisma-check`, `strobe-check`, `stard-check`, `tripod-check`, `care-check`, `arrive-check`, `qualitative-check`), or `checklist-check` for any other guideline.
