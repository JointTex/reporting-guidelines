---
name: stard-check
description: Check a diagnostic accuracy study against the STARD checklist and say where each item is reported.
---

Use this for a study that estimates how well a test identifies a target condition against a reference standard. For a multivariable prediction model use `tripod-check`.

## Get the checklist

1. Obtain the official STARD checklist: a file the collaborator adds to the project, or fetched with fetch_url from the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). Do not recite items from memory. If the fetch does not return a usable checklist (many are PDF or Word downloads), ask the collaborator to add the file to the project; do not go on from memory.
2. Ask whether an extension applies and obtain it too, for example the checklist for abstracts or the one for studies of AI-based tests.
3. Ask which version and which form the journal requires, and use the guideline's explanation and elaboration document when an item's meaning is unclear.

## Read

4. Read the whole manuscript, the participant flow diagram, every table and figure, and the supplementary files in the project.

## Assess

5. For each item, in the checklist's order, record: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable, with the location (section and paragraph, or page and line if the form asks for them) and a brief quotation or the table or figure that supports the judgment. "Partly reported" names what is missing; "not applicable" gives the reason.
6. Judge reporting, not conduct: an item is reported when the manuscript says what was done, including that something was not done. Do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Pay particular attention to:
   - the index test and the reference standard are each described in enough detail to repeat, with the rationale for the reference standard;
   - positivity cut-offs or result categories for both, and whether they were set before the study or chosen from the data;
   - whether the readers of the index test knew the reference standard result and the clinical information, and the reverse;
   - how participants were identified and sampled (consecutive, random or convenience), where and when, and the eligibility criteria;
   - the flow of participants: eligible, received the index test, received the reference standard, analysed, with reasons at each step, agreeing with the text and tables;
   - the cross tabulation of index test results against reference standard results can be reconstructed, and the reported sensitivity, specificity and predictive values follow from it;
   - indeterminate, missing and failed results on either test are counted and their handling stated;
   - accuracy estimates have their precision, and the time interval and any treatment between the two tests are given;
   - the severity and spectrum of the target condition and the alternative diagnoses in those without it;
   - the intended use and clinical role of the test, and conclusions that go no further than the population studied.

## Report

8. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, with the official item wording and order, then a short list of gaps, most important first, with the information needed for each, and separately any inconsistency found in step 7.
9. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides. Never invent study details such as cut-offs, counts, intervals, blinding arrangements or registration identifiers; ask for them and leave a marked placeholder.
10. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards, since page and line numbers move.
