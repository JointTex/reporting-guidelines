---
name: strobe-check
description: Check a cohort, case-control or cross-sectional study report against the STROBE checklist.
---

Use this for the report of an observational study. If the design is unclear, start with `guideline-select`.

## Get the checklist

1. Establish the design (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional) with the collaborator, since several items differ by design. If the manuscript's own description of its design does not match what was done (for example a study called a cohort that samples on the outcome), say so and ask before choosing.
2. Obtain the matching official checklist, or the combined one: a file the collaborator adds to the project, or fetched with fetch_url from the STROBE website (www.strobe-statement.org) or the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). Do not recite items from memory. If the fetch does not return a usable checklist, ask the collaborator to add the file; do not go on from memory.
3. Ask whether an extension applies and obtain it too, for example routinely collected health data, genetic association studies, Mendelian randomisation, molecular epidemiology, nutritional epidemiology, or conference abstracts.
4. Use the explanation and elaboration document when an item's meaning is unclear.

## Read

5. Read the whole manuscript, every table and figure, and the supplementary files in the project.

## Assess

6. For each item record: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable, with the location and a brief quotation. "Partly reported" names what is missing; "not applicable" gives the reason. Judge reporting, not conduct, and do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Pay particular attention to what readers need to judge bias:
   - setting, dates of recruitment, exposure, follow-up and data collection;
   - how participants were selected, with eligibility criteria and sources; for case-control studies how cases and controls were ascertained and matched; for cohort studies how follow-up was done;
   - how every outcome, exposure, predictor, confounder and effect modifier was defined and measured, and whether measurement was comparable between groups;
   - how the study size was arrived at;
   - how quantitative variables were handled and why groupings were chosen;
   - how confounding was addressed, how missing data were handled, how loss to follow-up or matching was dealt with in the analysis, and any sensitivity analyses.
8. Cross-checks:
   - numbers at each stage (potentially eligible, examined, confirmed eligible, included, followed up, analysed) are given, with reasons for non-participation, and agree between the text, any flow diagram and the tables;
   - the number with missing data is given for each variable of interest;
   - unadjusted and adjusted estimates are both given, with their precision, and the confounders adjusted for are named with the reason they were chosen;
   - causal language in the abstract and discussion goes no further than the design supports;
   - the limitations discuss the direction and likely size of bias, not only its existence.

## Report

9. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, with the official item wording and order, then a short list of gaps, most important first, and separately any inconsistency found in step 8.
10. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides; never invent study details, numbers or dates. Ask for them and leave a marked placeholder.
11. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards.
