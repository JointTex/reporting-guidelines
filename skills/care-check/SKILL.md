---
name: care-check
description: Check a case report against the CARE checklist, including the timeline, the patient perspective and consent.
---

Use this for a case report. CARE is written for the report of a single patient; for a case series, ask what the journal requires before using it.

## Get the checklist

1. Obtain the official CARE checklist: a file the collaborator adds to the project, or fetched with fetch_url from the CARE website (www.care-statement.org) or the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). Do not recite items from memory. If the fetch does not return a usable checklist (many are PDF or Word downloads), ask the collaborator to add the file to the project; do not go on from memory.
2. Ask whether an extension applies (some specialties and interventions have their own) and whether the journal has its own case report structure or word limit, and obtain those too.
3. Ask which version and which form the journal requires, and use the guideline's explanation and elaboration document when an item's meaning is unclear.

## Read

4. Read the whole manuscript, with its figures, images and tables (read_image for images when available).

## Assess

5. For each item, in the checklist's order, record: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable, with the location (section and paragraph, or page and line if the form asks for them) and a brief quotation or the table or figure that supports the judgment. "Partly reported" names what is missing; "not applicable" gives the reason.
6. Judge reporting, not conduct: an item is reported when the manuscript says what was done, including that something was not done. Do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Pay particular attention to:
   - the title and keywords identify it as a case report and name its focus;
   - what is unique about the case and what it adds is stated, with the supporting literature cited;
   - a timeline orders the relevant history, findings, interventions and outcomes, and the dates or intervals in it agree with the narrative;
   - diagnostic assessment includes the methods, the reasoning, the alternatives considered and any diagnostic challenge;
   - interventions are given with type, dose, duration and changes with their reasons;
   - follow-up and outcomes cover both clinician-assessed and patient-reported results, adherence and tolerability, and adverse or unanticipated events;
   - the discussion states strengths and limitations and does not generalise from one patient or assert causation the case cannot show;
   - the patient perspective is included where it could be obtained, and a statement that informed consent was given is present;
   - privacy: flag anything that could identify the patient (names, initials, exact dates, places, record numbers, rare combinations of details, faces, identifying marks or burned-in annotations in images). You cannot inspect the hidden metadata of image files, so remind the collaborator to strip it. Report these to the collaborator even where the checklist does not ask.

## Report

8. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, with the official item wording and order, then a short list of gaps, most important first, with the information needed for each, and separately any inconsistency found in step 7.
9. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides. Never invent clinical details, dates, test results, the patient's words or a consent statement; ask for them and leave a marked placeholder.
10. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards, since page and line numbers move.
