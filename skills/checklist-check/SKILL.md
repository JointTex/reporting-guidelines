---
name: checklist-check
description: Check a manuscript against any reporting checklist the collaborator supplies, item by item, with the location and evidence for each.
---

Use this for a reporting guideline that has no skill of its own in this set (for example CHEERS, SQUIRE, TREND, MOOSE, TIDieR, a journal's own checklist, or an extension used by itself), or when the collaborator hands over a checklist and says to check against it.

1. Obtain the checklist text: a file in the project (read_pdf for a PDF), text pasted into the conversation, or the official document fetched with fetch_url from the address the collaborator gives or from the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). Work only from the text you obtained. If you cannot obtain it, say so and stop; do not reconstruct a checklist from memory.
2. State which document you are using: its name, version or year, and where it came from. Ask whether the journal requires a particular version or form.
3. Read the checklist first and note for each item what it asks to be reported and where it expects it (title, abstract, methods and so on). Use the guideline's explanation document when an item is unclear, and ask the collaborator when it stays unclear.
4. Read the whole manuscript, with its tables, figures and the supplementary files in the project.
5. For each item, in the checklist's order and with its wording, record:
   - status: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable;
   - location: section and paragraph, or page and line if the form asks for them;
   - evidence: a brief quotation, or the table or figure;
   - for partly reported, what is missing; for not applicable, why.
6. Judge reporting, not conduct. An item is reported when the manuscript says what was done, including that something was not done. Do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Whatever the guideline, check that numbers agree between the abstract, the text, the tables and the figures, and that the abstract states nothing the main text does not.
8. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, then a short list of gaps, most important first, with the information needed for each.
9. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides. Never invent study details, numbers, dates, approvals or registration identifiers; ask for them and leave a marked placeholder.
10. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards, since page and line numbers move.
