---
name: prisma-check
description: Check a systematic review or meta-analysis against the PRISMA checklist and flow diagram.
---

Use this for a systematic review, with or without meta-analysis. If the kind of review is unclear, start with `guideline-select`.

## Get the checklist

1. Ask for the PRISMA version the journal requires and obtain the official text: a file the collaborator adds to the project, or the checklist fetched with fetch_url from the PRISMA website (www.prisma-statement.org) or the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). Do not recite items from memory. If the fetch does not return a usable checklist, ask the collaborator to add the file; do not go on from memory.
2. Ask which extension applies and obtain it too: scoping reviews, network meta-analysis, individual participant data, diagnostic test accuracy, protocols, searching, harms, equity, and the checklist for abstracts. A scoping review or a protocol is checked against its own extension. Extension items are otherwise checked in addition to the main checklist.
3. Use the explanation and elaboration document when an item's meaning is unclear.

## Read

4. Read the manuscript, the full search strategy, the flow diagram, the tables of study characteristics and risk of bias, the forest plots, and the supplementary files in the project. If the collaborator supplies the registration record or the protocol, read them.

## Assess

5. For each item record: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable, with the location and a brief quotation. "Partly reported" names what is missing; "not applicable" gives the reason (for example synthesis items in a review without meta-analysis).
6. Judge reporting, not conduct, and do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Cross-checks:
   - the flow diagram adds up at every stage: records identified per source, duplicates removed, screened, excluded, reports sought and not retrieved, assessed for eligibility, excluded with reasons, studies and reports included; and it agrees with the text;
   - the number of included studies equals the rows of the characteristics table, the studies in the risk-of-bias table or figure, and the studies accounted for in the syntheses;
   - the search can be repeated: every database and other source named, the date each was last searched, limits and filters, and the full search strategies to the extent the version of the checklist you obtained requires (earlier versions asked for less than the current one, so read the item and do not assume);
   - eligibility criteria in the methods match the reasons for exclusion in the flow diagram;
   - the risk-of-bias tool is named and its results are shown per study, not only summarised;
   - each synthesis states the effect measure, the model, how heterogeneity was assessed, and gives estimates with their precision; subgroup and sensitivity analyses are identified as prespecified or not;
   - certainty of evidence is reported for each main outcome if the review claims to have assessed it;
   - registration and protocol are stated, with deviations from the protocol, and the abstract agrees with the main text.

## Report

8. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, with the official item wording and order, then a short list of gaps, most important first, and separately any inconsistency found in step 7.
9. Do not invent search dates, record counts, registration numbers, risk-of-bias judgments or certainty ratings; ask for them and leave a marked placeholder. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides.
10. If the flow diagram is missing or wrong, offer `flow-diagram`. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards.
