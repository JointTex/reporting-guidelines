---
name: spirit-check
description: Check a clinical trial protocol against the SPIRIT checklist and say where each item is addressed.
---

Use this for the protocol of a clinical trial, whether a protocol paper or the protocol document itself. For the report of a completed trial use `consort-check`.

## Get the checklist

1. Obtain the official SPIRIT checklist: a file the collaborator adds to the project, or fetched with fetch_url from the SPIRIT website or the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). The statement has been revised (the 2013 and 2025 versions differ in items and numbering), so ask which one the journal or funder requires, and state which you used. Do not recite items from memory. If the fetch does not return a usable checklist (many are PDF or Word downloads), ask the collaborator to add the file to the project; do not go on from memory.
2. Ask whether an extension applies and obtain it too, for example patient-reported outcomes, AI interventions, outcomes, n-of-1 trials or traditional medicine, and whether the intervention should also be described to TIDieR.
3. Ask which version and which form the journal requires, and use the guideline's explanation and elaboration document when an item's meaning is unclear.

## Read

4. Read the whole protocol with its appendices: the schedule of enrolment, interventions and assessments, the consent materials if they are in the project, and the registry record if the collaborator supplies it.

## Assess

5. For each item, in the checklist's order, record: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable, with the location (section and paragraph, or page and line if the form asks for them) and a brief quotation or the table or figure that supports the judgment. "Partly reported" names what is missing; "not applicable" gives the reason.
6. Judge reporting, not conduct: an item is addressed when the protocol says what will be done, including that something will not be done. Do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Pay particular attention to:
   - the schedule figure or table: every assessment named in the text appears in it at the stated time points, and the reverse;
   - objectives, primary and secondary outcomes each have a measurement, a metric, a method of aggregation and a time point, and they are the same in the summary, the body and the registry record;
   - the sample size section states every assumption needed to repeat the calculation, and the number matches the recruitment target stated elsewhere;
   - sequence generation, allocation concealment and implementation are each described separately, and who is blinded and how;
   - the statistical methods name the analysis population, the handling of missing data, and any interim analysis with its stopping guidelines;
   - data monitoring, harms collection, auditing, protocol amendments, consent, confidentiality, access to data and the dissemination policy are each addressed or stated as not applicable with a reason;
   - protocol version and date, registration, funder, sponsor and roles are stated;
   - the tense: a protocol states what will be done. Flag passages written as if results existed.

## Report

8. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, with the official item wording and order, then a short list of gaps, most important first, with the information needed for each, and separately any inconsistency found in step 7.
9. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides. Never invent design details such as sample size assumptions, randomisation methods, outcome definitions, committee membership, version numbers, dates or registration identifiers; ask for them and leave a marked placeholder.
10. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards, since page and line numbers move.
