---
name: arrive-check
description: Check a report of animal research against the ARRIVE guidelines, starting with the Essential 10.
---

Use this for a study that reports experiments on animals.

## Get the checklist

1. Obtain the official ARRIVE checklist: a file the collaborator adds to the project, or fetched with fetch_url from the ARRIVE website (www.arriveguidelines.org) or the EQUATOR Network (www.equator-network.org). The current version divides its items into an essential set and a recommended set; ask which the journal requires, check the essential set first, and state which version you used. Do not recite items from memory. If the fetch does not return a usable checklist (many are PDF or Word downloads), ask the collaborator to add the file to the project; do not go on from memory.
2. Ask whether the journal has its own form (some require the completed author checklist or a compliance questionnaire) and obtain it too.
3. Ask which version and which form the journal requires, and use the guideline's explanation and elaboration document when an item's meaning is unclear.

## Read

4. Read the whole manuscript, every figure legend and table, and the supplementary files in the project. Much of what the guidelines ask for sits in methods subsections and figure legends.

## Assess

5. For each item, in the checklist's order, record: reported, partly reported, not reported, or not applicable, with the location (section and paragraph, or page and line if the form asks for them) and a brief quotation or the table or figure that supports the judgment. "Partly reported" names what is missing; "not applicable" gives the reason.
6. Judge reporting, not conduct: an item is reported when the manuscript says what was done, including that something was not done. Do not infer a step from usual practice.
7. Pay particular attention to:
   - each experiment is identified with its groups, including controls, and the experimental unit is named (animal, cage, litter): the unit in the design matches the unit in the analysis;
   - the exact number of units in each group for each experiment and analysis, how the sample size was decided, and whether that was done beforehand;
   - inclusion and exclusion criteria, whether they were set in advance, and every animal or data point excluded with the reason; group sizes in the results agree with the methods;
   - whether allocation was randomised and by what method, and how confounders such as order of treatment or cage position were handled;
   - who was aware of group allocation at each stage: allocation, conduct, outcome assessment, analysis;
   - each outcome measure is defined, and the primary one is named where a hypothesis was tested;
   - statistical methods are given per analysis, with the software and how assumptions were checked;
   - the animals: species, strain and substrain, sex, age or developmental stage, weight where relevant, source, and genetic modification;
   - procedures in enough detail to repeat: what, how, when, where, with anaesthesia and analgesia;
   - results give summary statistics with a measure of variability for each group and, where applicable, an effect size with its precision, and say which bars or bands in each figure show which;
   - ethical approval with the authority and licence or protocol number, housing and husbandry, monitoring and humane endpoints, and adverse events.

## Report

8. Produce the completed checklist as a table in the form the journal asks for, with the official item wording and order, then a short list of gaps, most important first, with the information needed for each, and separately any inconsistency found in step 7.
9. Suggest wording only from what the manuscript or the collaborator provides. Never invent experimental details such as group sizes, randomisation or blinding procedures, strain names, doses, approval or licence numbers; ask for them and leave a marked placeholder.
10. Edit the manuscript only when asked, and update the locations in the checklist afterwards, since page and line numbers move.
